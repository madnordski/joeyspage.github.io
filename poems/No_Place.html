No Place

The stream does not ask me to leave. It simply goes on. Cold over stone, stone over earth, earth settling by fractions of an inch through centuries I cannot feel. Earth correcting river, a flow it did not choose. The path bends, it twists, gathering bugs and branch, ever changing but always, in conversation with the earth. I stand beside it for a moment, and I am the temporary thing. The water has no need of my witness. It moves on. The days are marked by clocks, by coffee in the morning, by laundry and groceries, by appointments and obligations, by email recieved and returned. There is a place for everything. A chair at the table. A sink by the window. A bike hung on the wall. A step out the door. A rigid wall in the garden. Yet even there, among all the boxes that enclose us, I sometimes feel a compelling pull, like the current moving beneath the stream. The world shaping movement. Of time. Of place. Of people. The world is constantly correcting. And somewhere between its motion and my own a distance opens, small at first, then a gaping hole. A separation. Not loneliness exactly. Something older. Something harder to name. So I leave. The bicycle is loaded. Food. Water. Tools. Tent. Sleeping bag. Everything required for a temporary existence. Then I turn pedals. Road becomes gravel. Gravel becomes forest. Forest becomes ridge and valley, River crossings and big skys. The rhythm of tires over stone replaces the rhythm of conversation. And for a while I imagine I am entering a place where I belong more completely. A place beyond the noise. A place beyond the endless correction of human intention. But the wild country has its own ways of telling me what's real. The deer lift their heads. Motionless. Watching. Their ears turn toward me, catching the faint hiss of tires, the click of a freewheel, the sound of an unfamiliar creature moving through familiar ground. For a moment we share the same air. The same sunlight. The same silence. Then they bound away. White tails flashing. A series of effortless leaps. And I am left behind, watching the space where they disappeared. Then something else flashes across the road, The white tip of the tail gives it away. A fox, A brief stroke of color painted across the road. One glance. One instant. Then it vanishes. Not away from danger. Away from me. Into shadow. Into brush. Into a world where every root and scent already knows its name. Sometimes the smell of bear hangs heavy in the air. Musky. Garbage. Pungent. The scent hangs heavy, lingering long after it has passed. A reminder. A signature. A presence. Evidence of lives unfolding beyond the limits of my sight. Then comes the crack of branches. The sound of uncaring weight moving through timber. The forest speaking in a language I struggle to understand. The crushing fades. The silence returns. I turn pedals. Always continuing. The gravel crunches beneath my wheels. An answer of my own, small and out of place. The farther I travel, the more obvious it becomes. Nothing here has been waiting for me. The rivers flow. The birds sing. The trees breath. The earth conducts. Everything belongs. Everything participates. Everything is woven into a harmony established long before my appearance. I move through it. But I do not join it. At dusk I stop. Not because I have arrived. Only because darkness has. The tent rises. Poles bend. Stakes bite earth. A small island appears. Fabric walls. Zippers. Nylon. A manufactured shelter placed briefly upon an indiffernt earth. Inside, I crawl into another layer. Sleeping bag. Pad. Pillow. A cocoon within a cocoon. Food secured. Odors hidden. Distance maintained. The rituals of coexistence. Necessary. Wise. And strangely revealing. Even here, where I have traveled so far to escape the structures of ordinary life, I construct another structure. Another boundary. Another separation. A correction. Outside, the night proceeds without me. Wind moves through pine needles. Owls exchange their questions. Leaves turn their pale undersides toward passing air. Somewhere, a deer steps softly through darkness. Somewhere, a fox follows a scent. Somewhere, a bear overturns a log in search of grubs. The forest continues. Whole. Complete. Indifferent. And I lie awake inside layers of fabric and insulation, listening. Present. Yet apart. Morning comes. Condensation beads on the fly. The zipper opens. Light enters. The world is still there. The stream still runs. The deer still watch. The fox still disappears. The bear still leaves its scent upon the wind. And still I return to turning pedals. Still point the wheel down another road. Still move through landscapes that neither welcome nor reject me. Perhaps that is the lesson. Not that I am excluded. Not that I am abandoned. Not that there is hostility between myself and the world. Only that belonging is not the same as passing through. The stream belongs. The deer belong. The fox belongs. The bear belongs. The spruce, the stone, the river, the rain. They are of the place. I am movement. I am transit. I am the brief disturbance of tires on gravel. A shadow crossing a clearing. A tent appearing at dusk and disappearing at dawn. A traveler. Nothing more. And so the miles accumulate. The endless stream continues corrected by the earth. Water shaping stone. Time shaping memory. Distance shaping understanding. I pedal onward through the wild country, carrying everything I need, carrying everything that separates me. The deer stare and vanish. The fox flashes across the road. The scent of bear drifts through timber. Branches crack somewhere beyond sight. The gravel answers beneath my wheels. And all around me, life continues in its ancient conversation. A conversation I can hear. A conversation I can admire. A conversation I can never join. I move through here. I always move through here. Never of here. Never part of here. And in that realization, clear as cold water over stone, I find there is no place for me.
— Joey King
